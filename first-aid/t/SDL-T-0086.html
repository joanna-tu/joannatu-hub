When a spending freeze stops your deal, ask what still gets approved

A freeze is a written policy with an exception lane. Ask what it exempts, what has been approved since it began and who signed. The library's own move; untested in selling.

Evidence: assembled by the library. Nobody publishes this as one move. The library put it together from parts that are published.

What it is.

The buyer's company has announced a spending freeze, and your deal stopped on the word.

Treat the freeze as a written policy, not a wall. Ask what it exempts and what has been approved since it started: which purchases went through, who signed them, and what the person asking had to provide. What comes back is either a lane, with a threshold, a list of exempt categories and a named approver, or nothing the buyer can point to. Either answer tells you where the deal stands.

No seller publishes this question. The move is the library's own, built on the fact that freeze documents usually write down their own exceptions.

What it looks like.

The nearest sales advice changes the contract, not the approval route. Ian Koniak's post on no-budget and spending-freeze objections teaches a future-dated order, signed now with the start date written in for later, and a split payment, half now and half in six months. Neither asks what the freeze lets through.

Washington State University's spending pause. From 1 April 2025, any purchase or contract of $10,000 or more needs approval through an exception review. Outside it sit anything under $10,000, purchases on designated grant accounts, travel paid by federal research funds, and purchases needed to protect life, property or public safety. The person asking fills in a questionnaire on why the purchase is essential. Requests from academic departments go through the dean, the provost and the executive vice president for finance.

The University of Oregon's hiring and pay freeze. The notice lists what the freeze covers, from faculty and staff hiring to stipends and pay rises, and what it does not: reappointments to the same role go ahead, as do the September 2026 merit increases. And departments may request exceptions.

Where it has been tested.

In B2B sales

Nobody has tested it. No study looks at what happens when a supplier asks what has been approved since a freeze began, or compares sellers who worked the exception lane with sellers who waited for the freeze to lift.

In other disciplines

What an audit of freezes found. The US General Accounting Office reviewed four government-wide hiring freezes from 1977 to 1981 in a report published in March 1982. All four had written exemptions, starting with jobs involving the safety of human life, and all four let agency heads appeal to the budget office for more. During the freeze from March 1980 to January 1981, full-time permanent employment fell by 8,874, while part-time and temporary employment, which that freeze did not cover, rose by 18,626. GAO put most of that rise down to seasonal hiring and raised ceilings, not to the freeze itself, but it also found agencies filling the gap with temporary staff, contractors and overtime.

The lane exists and is not reliably open. The same report found the budget office's records of exemption decisions incomplete, and some decisions driven by headcount targets rather than need. One department secretary asked to fill 75% of certain vacancies and was refused because it would endanger the President's targets. Another agency asked for a blanket exemption for appointing veterans and disabled people, and was refused for the same reason.

Caveat.

The measured case is one audit of US federal hiring freezes forty years ago, which counts jobs, not purchases. Nobody has measured what a supplier learns by asking. And a lane existing says nothing about your chances in it: the same audit shows requests refused to protect a headcount target.

Takeaway.

Ask what the freeze exempts, what has been approved since it began, who signed it and what the person asking had to provide. The published freeze documents show the lane is usually written down, with a threshold, exempt categories and a named approver, so the answer is often a matter of record, not a favour. Treat a live lane as a route worth testing, not as a reason to put the deal back in the forecast.
