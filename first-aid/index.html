Silent, cause unknown.

They stopped replying, and you do not know why.

Make it easy for a silent buyer to say no

Send

“Have you given up on solving this problem?” as the subject line, and nothing else.

From Chris Voss
Never

Tell them you are closing their file.

Guess what happened. Let them correct you.

Send

Your best guess at their week in two ordinary sentences, such as “The proposal is sitting with finance.” Then give them a one-word way to answer.

Not when

Your guess is about what they think of you. Guess about the process only.

Go back when something changes on their side

Do

Wait for a change on their side, such as a new executive, a reorganisation or a competitor’s contract coming up for renewal. Write because of it, with something they can use even if they never buy.

Not when

The only reason to write is your own calendar.

2

Not convinced it matters.

They agree the problem exists, and it does not feel big enough to act on.

Build the business case with the buyer, including the cost of doing nothing

Do

Work out with them, from their own figures, what carrying on as they are costs. Put staying put on the slide as its own option, before the demo.

Not when

It is a renewal, or the buyer is already convinced and has gone quiet.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

When they plan to build it with AI, run their own build-or-buy test with them

Ask

Is there a product that already fits? Does it touch customer data or payments? Who maintains it after launch?

From the build-or-buy test Jason Lemkin publishes
Not when

You mean to argue that they cannot build it. Where the answer really is build, let that part go and sell the rest.

3

Convinced but afraid.

They want it, and they are afraid of getting it wrong.

When the buyer keeps asking for more, ask what it is for

Ask

What is this for, and what will it change in your decision? When the same request comes back, describe what you see in flat words, then stop talking.

Not when

You have not earned their trust yet. The authors say this step only works on trust built earlier.

Evidence: vendor data. Read the entry

Take the risk off the table

Do

Find out what your champion stands to lose. Then shrink the first step until they could survive it failing, such as a pilot with success criteria and a price agreed before it starts.

Not when

The buyer still doubts the problem. This one is for a buyer who is convinced and scared.

Evidence: vendor data. Read the entry

Offer one recommendation, not a menu

Say

Which option you would pick, why, and what you would leave out for now.

Not when

They are still comparing options. Keep the menu for the evaluation and the recommendation for the approval.

4

Cannot get it through the organisation.

Your contact is convinced, and the rest of their company is not moving.

The mutual action plan

Do

Write a short plan back from the day they want to be live, with a name and a date on every step on both sides, and the contract as one step in the middle.

Not when

The dates come from your quarter end, or the plan runs past fifteen steps.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

Ghostwrite what the champion has to send

Do

Write the meeting request, the one-page business case and the note to the group yourself, and hand them over as rough drafts to edit.

Not when

The drafts carry your branding or your pitch. Buyers discount help that reads as promotion.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

Ask who decides if the committee cannot agree

Ask

If you have not agreed by date, who decides?

Not when

It is the last minute. Ask early enough to check the name against what that person actually does.

5

No reason to act now.

Everyone agrees, and nobody needs it this quarter.

Ask what it would take

Ask

“What would it take?” Write their conditions down and send them back as the plan.

From Zoe Chance
Not when

You are still arguing. If they start defending how things work today, stop and ask.

Evidence: studies from another field. Read the entry

Park with a trigger

Say

“I hope this gets done and I’ll work towards it. It’s also possible it won’t. How much is it worth to you right now?” Then offer two paths: go ahead by a date, or park until a named event.

From the library’s rewrite of a line from Ariadne Labs’ conversation guide
Never

Say “let’s stay in touch” or “I’m closing your file”.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

When they wait for their AI strategy, ask what it has to decide

Ask

What will the AI strategy decide, who owns it, and when does it land? Which of those decisions changes this purchase?

Not when

The strategy has no owner and no date. Then park the deal with a trigger instead of waiting.

6

Stuck in the machinery.

They said yes, and now legal, security or procurement have it.

Get the contract through legal and security

Ask

How many reviews are ahead of ours, and how many arrive each week? Before the contract goes out, date every redline step with your champion.

Never

Present the stops as trouble. Name them as normal steps.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

Trade, don’t give

Say

“We can look at 10% off if you commit to two years”, never “we can do 10% off”.

Never

Give a discount for nothing, or invent an expiry.

7

The people or company changed.

Your champion left, a new executive arrived, or the money froze.

When your champion leaves, hand the successor the case and the plan

Send

The business case and the mutual action plan, as the record of a project already running. Then test the successor as a champion from scratch.

Never

Assume the successor inherits the conviction. They inherit the job title.

Evidence: assembled by the library. Read the entry

When a new executive arrives, open with an insight, not a re-pitch

Say

“It seems like company is focused on X.” Then ask what you got wrong.

From a Gong talk track
Never

Send a shorter version of the old pitch. They never approved it.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

When a spending freeze stops your deal, ask what still gets approved

Ask

What does the freeze exempt, and what has it let through since it began?

Never

Put the deal back in the forecast just because an exception lane exists.

8

Existing-customer dynamics.

It is a customer, and the renewal has gone quiet or they want to pay less.

When a renewal goes quiet, start with what they achieved

Do

Book a call 90 to 120 days out on their results and next year’s goals, with everyone who has a say. Raise the renewal after that.

Never

Open with the renewal.

Evidence: documented practice. Read the entry

When the customer wants to pay less

Ask

What changed? Then trade scope, term or payment timing for price.

Never

Offer the cheaper plan first. In a field experiment it made more customers leave.

9

We caused it.

Something you did stopped the deal.

When your mistake stalled the deal, name it and lead with the fix

Do

Name the mistake, then put the fix first and the regret last.

Not when

You are accused of dishonesty you did not commit. Then deny it firmly instead of apologising.

Evidence: study-backed. Read the entry

Ask for their advice

Ask

How would you word this for your finance team? One named problem, asked personally.

Not when

It is about something they are weak at. Asking about that made the asker look worse.
